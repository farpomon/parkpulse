“Can’t I just use the official app?”

For live waits, yes — and you should have it open. It is the only place that can sell you the pass, book your slot and order your food. Here is what it will never do.

It sells the pass

Every skip-the-line pass is sold by the park that runs the queue. An app on that side of the counter has no reason to tell you today is a day to save your money — and it never will. We have nothing to sell you at the gate, so “skip it today” is an answer we can actually give.

It knows one resort

Each operator’s app covers that operator’s parks. A trip that mixes a Disney day, a Universal day and a coaster park means three apps and no shared plan. ParkPulse holds all 65 in one place, with one plan across the whole trip.

A number is not advice

“45 min” tells you the length of the line. It does not tell you whether 45 is good, whether it drops after 4pm, or what to ride while you wait. We snapshot every ride every 15 minutes and compare today against that ride’s own two-week median, so we can say 20 minutes below typical — go now.

We are not a replacement for the official app and we don’t pretend to be. Book inside it. Decide with us.

Pay for the trip, not a subscription

Every pass includes all 65 parks in twenty languages, the plan builder, your magical fairy and wait-drop alerts, on every device in your family. One Lightning Lane costs $15–$45 per person, per day.

Questions, answered straight

Is ParkPulse free?

Yes, to start. Free forever: the live wait board for Magic Kingdom, one Mila-built day plan every day, all 500+ ready-made touring plans, and every park's crowd calendar and strategy guide. One-time passes from $6.99 unlock live waits and unlimited planning across all 65 parks.

What is Mila?

Mila is ParkPulse's AI park fairy. She builds your whole day in one tap around live waits and crowd forecasts, re-plans when the day changes, answers questions like “is the skip pass worth it today?” — and tells you when to keep your money.

How accurate are the wait times?

Live waits are the parks' own posted times via Queue-Times.com, refreshed about every minute. Forecasts are built from historical patterns per ride and hour — they are estimates, and we publish our accuracy record openly.

Which parks does ParkPulse cover?

65 parks across five regions: every Walt Disney World and Disneyland park, Universal Orlando, Hollywood and Japan, Six Flags, LEGOLAND, Busch Gardens, SeaWorld, Cedar Point, Europa-Park, Efteling, Everland, Lotte World and more. See the full list.

Is it a subscription?

No. Passes are one-time purchases — a day, a trip, a season or a year — and nothing auto-renews. Full refund within 14 days if the pass hasn't been materially used.

Can it plan around my child’s height?

Yes. Give your child’s height once and any ride they would be turned away from is left out of the plan entirely — so you never queue for something they cannot board. Mixed groups still get the big rides: ParkPulse knows which attractions run rider switch, so one adult rides while the other waits with the little one, then they swap.

Does the plan change for the weather?

It does. A hot afternoon moves you onto water rides and into air-conditioned attractions at the peak hour instead of leaving you in an exposed queue; rain pulls the plan towards indoor rides and shows, and tells you to pack the poncho. It also knows when sunset is, because the half hour before it is the best light you will get for photographs.

We are going with grandparents — does that change anything?

Yes. An older guest in the group slows the walking pace between stops, builds a real sit-down into the afternoon, and moves gentler rides and shows up the running order — without taking the thrill rides away from everyone else. The same applies in reverse for a group of teenagers.

What is a touring plan?

An hour-by-hour running order for a park day: what to ride first, when to eat, when the lines dip. ParkPulse publishes 500+ free touring plans — for families with little ones, thrill seekers, rainy days and more — and each one opens live in the app, where it re-times itself against today's actual waits.
